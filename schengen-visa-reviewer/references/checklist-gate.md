# Official document checklist gate

Run this before the four goal-based review modules.

## Confirm that this version can review the case

Continue only when the main purpose is short-stay tourism. Multi-country tourist trips are supported. If the application is business, visiting family or friends, study, work, medical treatment, airport transit, long stay, or another purpose, stop before grading and tell the user that the current version supports short-stay Schengen tourism only. Never reuse the tourism checklist for an unsupported purpose.

If the purpose is unclear, ask the user to confirm the main purpose. If it is tourism but the responsible country or submission jurisdiction cannot yet be identified, request only the facts needed to route the case.

## Identify the exact checklist

Determine:

- Schengen Member State responsible for the application;
- visa purpose and subtype;
- country and city or consular jurisdiction of submission;
- applicant nationality and lawful residence;
- adult or minor, employed, self-employed, student, retired, unemployed, sponsored, or travelling with family;
- any special category such as EU/EEA family member, business, visiting family, medical treatment, or airport transit.

Use these facts to decide either `Accepted for tourism review` or `Outside current scope`. For an accepted case, show this identification block at the beginning of the report:

- identified application: `[responsible country] short-stay Schengen tourism visa`;
- destinations and travel dates;
- expected responsible Member State and a short reason;
- place of submission and lawful residence basis;
- official checklist title, link, and access date;
- checklist status: `Confirmed for this case` or `Provisional — exact checklist not verified`.

Example: when the user is applying for a French tourist visa, route the checklist check to France's official visa guidance and the French authority or appointed centre serving the actual submission location. Do not reject the case merely because the built-in baseline happens to cover the Netherlands.

The short-stay application form is harmonised at EU level, but portals may provide localised or electronic versions. Supporting-document requirements are not safely interchangeable across every Member State, purpose, and submission jurisdiction. Never use a Netherlands checklist as proof of requirements for France, Germany, or another country.

## Source priority

1. Responsible Member State's government, embassy, or consulate.
2. EU legal document or locally harmonised supporting-document list.
3. The responsible authority's official visa portal.
4. The officially appointed visa application centre, checked against government instructions.

Record URL, title, version or publication date when available, and access date. Do not treat a blog, social post, generic agency list, or another country's checklist as authority.

If the exact current checklist cannot be found, label the result **Provisional — official checklist not verified**. Do not give an A grade or say the package is complete.

## Build the comparison table

For every applicable checklist item, record:

- official requirement in plain language;
- status: **Provided**, **Partly provided**, **Missing**, **Not applicable**, **Unreadable**, or **Unable to verify**;
- document or page that appears to satisfy it;
- formal defect such as stale date, missing signature, wrong format, incomplete pages, insufficient validity, or a document that expires before submission;
- exact action.

Distinguish a missing document from a weak document. A flight hold that expires before submission is not “missing,” but it may fail to prove the required booking. A generic employment letter is not the same as an employer confirmation of approved holiday when the checklist specifically asks for the latter.

Do not mark a requirement satisfied from a filename alone. Inspect the document. Do not infer that a document does not exist merely because it was not supplied; say “not supplied for review.”

## Grade effect

- Missing or unusable verified mandatory documents normally block an A grade and may create a Critical or High finding depending on materiality.
- Partly provided items require a concrete explanation of what is present and what is still missing.
- Optional improvements do not count as missing mandatory documents.
- A consulate's ability to request additional documents does not make every imaginable document mandatory.
