# Review matrix

Use the relevant rows; do not demand inapplicable documents.

| Area | Compare | Typical issue | Safe recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | Passport, form, bookings, insurance | spelling or passport-number mismatch | correct at the issuer; do not edit evidence manually |
| Jurisdiction | destinations, nights, purpose, first entry | wrong Member State | verify main-purpose or main-destination rule |
| Passport | issue date, expiry, blank pages, signature | fails current validity rule | renew or verify against current checklist |

## Date-table minimum fields

Record timestamps and time zones when known: departure from country of residence; first Schengen arrival and border entry; each accommodation check-in and check-out; internal transport that changes country or overnight location; final Schengen border exit; arrival back in the country of residence; insurance start and end; approved leave start and end; and application-form arrival and departure dates.

Flag both uncovered periods and differences that need explanation. Distinguish the date of arrival home from the date of departure from Schengen.

## Itinerary plausibility

Check each day door to door rather than adding only attraction durations. Include travel to the station, security or check-in where relevant, transfers, queues, meals, rest, hotel check-in, and a reasonable disruption buffer. Verify time-sensitive facts with official attraction and transport sources. Do not require every attraction to be prepaid or reserved when the operator does not require it.

“Special-forces-style” or highly compressed travel is a cultural travel preference, not an automatic credibility defect. Label it EXPERIENCE and flag only concrete consequences: overlapping bookings, impossible transfers, repeated cross-country day trips, no accommodation after a late arrival, or a schedule that cannot be executed at the stated opening times. Recommend a more realistic plan when simplification makes the application easier to understand and more truthful.

## Financial reconciliation

List each submitted payslip and locate the corresponding bank credit when possible. A missing credit is not automatically a defect: payroll timing, use of another account, or statement cutoff may explain it. Ask for the true reason and suggest proportionate corroboration. Do not recommend artificial transfers, borrowed balances presented as savings, or concealment of transaction history.
